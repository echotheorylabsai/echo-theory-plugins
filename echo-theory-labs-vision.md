## The Problem

The gap between what AI can do and what companies can ship is not closing. It's widening with every breakthrough.

Over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027 — driven by escalating costs, unclear value, and inadequate risk controls ([Gartner, June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). The failure pattern is consistent: teams ship agent systems fast, then discover they have no evaluation pipeline, no drift detection, no containment model, and no plan for what happens when the system degrades under real load. Only 21% of enterprises have a mature governance model for autonomous agents ([Deloitte, "State of AI in the Enterprise," January 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026.html)). Nearly 30% run zero evaluation on their agent systems ([LangChain, "State of AI Agents," 2026](https://www.langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering)). Legacy system integration remains a fundamental obstacle to production deployment, with organizations finding that traditional enterprise systems were never designed for agentic interactions ([Deloitte, 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/technology-management/tech-trends/2026/agentic-ai-strategy.html)).

The models are capable. The engineering discipline to make them reliable, secure, and production-grade barely exists.

## Built for What Comes Next

Today it's LLMs and agentic orchestration. The next production gaps are already visible — and we're already working on them.

**Domain-tuned small models** are replacing general-purpose LLMs where cost, latency, and data sovereignty matter. Enterprises are shifting toward model portfolio strategies — frontier models for complex reasoning, fine-tuned small models for production workloads, edge-deployed models for privacy-sensitive and latency-critical environments. The engineering discipline for managing that heterogeneous stack at scale does not exist yet. *We're prototyping evaluation and orchestration frameworks for multi-model stacks — testing how domain-tuned models behave under the same reliability standards we apply to frontier deployments.*

**Self-evolving agent systems** that learn from every interaction, outcome, and feedback loop are moving from research into early production. Closed-loop agents that detect their own workflow gaps, generate new procedures, and test improvements before deployment represent a fundamental shift — from systems that degrade after deployment to systems that improve. The discipline for making that loop safe, auditable, and reliable in high-stakes environments does not exist yet. *We're building early prototypes of closed-loop improvement systems with audit trails and safety boundaries — stress-testing what "self-improving" means when failure isn't an option.*

**World models and spatial reasoning** will extend agents beyond text into physical and visual environments — enabling AI systems that perceive, plan, and act in three-dimensional space. The production engineering for these systems does not exist yet either. *We're tracking frontier research closely and evaluating where our production methodology applies as these capabilities mature.*

Each of these gaps will demand new disciplines. The methodology for closing them stays the same: deep software engineering married to frontier research, applied before the gap becomes a crisis. Every engagement sharpens the framework. Every framework improvement accelerates the next engagement. Every case study published makes the next client easier to reach — and every new capability shift restarts the cycle.
