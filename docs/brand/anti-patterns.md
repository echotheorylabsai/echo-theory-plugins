# Echo Theory Labs — Voice Anti-Patterns

Canonical "never" list. Read by `draft-content` (to avoid) and `brand-review` (to flag). Each rule includes a one-line rationale so edge cases are judgable.

---

- **No hype superlatives without evidence** ("industry-leading", "best-in-class", "most advanced", "unparalleled", "#1"). — *Echo's authority comes from cited evidence, not adjectives. A superlative without a source reads as marketing puffery to the technical-peer audience.*
- **No fake-personal-story openers** ("Last quarter our team was spending 20 hours a week on..."). — *This is a marketing-blog idiom that signals "ad pretending to be a story" to engineers. Echo opens with a load-bearing claim, statistic, or named practice area instead.*
- **No lead-gen CTAs** ("Start your free trial", "No credit card required", "Book a demo to learn more"). — *Echo distributes research, not funnels. Lead-gen language collapses Echo into a SaaS vendor frame and breaks the lab positioning.*
- **No "we're excited to announce" energy.** — *Announcement-marketing tone undermines technical-peer register. State what changed and why it matters; the reader does not need our enthusiasm.*
- **No exclamation marks in production copy.** — *Exclamation marks signal excitement, not authority. Methodology notes do not exclaim.*
- **No disparaging a named competitor.** — *Cite frameworks and tools by name only to describe a technical pattern, never to mock or contrast unfavorably. Disparagement reads as insecurity to the audience.*
- **No unqualified absolutes** ("guaranteed", "no risk", "always", "never fails"). — *Production AI does not yield absolutes. Unqualified language signals the author has not run the system at scale.*
- **No "context engineering" as a pillar name.** — *Superseded by Agent Harness Engineering, which is the broader and current framing. Using the deprecated term signals stale positioning.*
